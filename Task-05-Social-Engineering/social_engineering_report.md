# Task 5: Research Report on Social Engineering Attacks

##  Objective
The objective of this task is to research the psychological and technical methods used in social engineering, analyze real-world examples, and propose robust defense mechanisms.

---

##  1. Types of Social Engineering Attacks

###  Phishing
**Description:** The practice of sending fraudulent communications that appear to come from a reputable source, usually through email.
* **How it works:** Attackers use "urgent" or "threatening" language to trick users into clicking malicious links or downloading malware.
* **Mitigation:** Implement email filtering, use Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA), and conduct regular employee awareness training.

###  Pretexting
**Description:** A situation where an attacker lies to obtain privileged data. It involves a fabricated scenario (the pretext) to establish trust.
* **How it works:** An attacker might pretend to be from the IT department or an external auditor to ask for login credentials or social security numbers.
* **Mitigation:** Establish strict identity verification protocols for all internal and external requests for information.

###  Baiting
**Description:** Similar to phishing, but it promises an item or good to entice the victim.
* **How it works:** An attacker might leave a malware-infected USB drive in a public place (like a lobby) labeled "Executive Salaries," hoping a curious employee will plug it in.
* **Mitigation:** Disable "AutoRun" features on company computers and prohibit the use of unapproved external media.

---

##  Case Study: The 2020 Twitter "Vishing" Attack
In July 2020, hackers successfully targeted several Twitter employees through a **Voice Phishing (Vishing)** campaign. By posing as internal IT help desk staff, the attackers convinced employees to provide their credentials for internal administrative tools.
* **Result:** The attackers hijacked high-profile accounts (including those of Barack Obama, Joe Biden, and Elon Musk) to promote a Bitcoin scam.
* **Lesson Learned:** Even the most secure technical environments are vulnerable to human manipulation.

---

##  Best Practices for Prevention
1. **Never Share Credentials:** No legitimate IT department will ever ask for your password.
2. **Think Before You Click:** Hover over links to see the actual destination URL before clicking.
3. **Report Suspicious Activity:** Encourage a culture where employees feel safe reporting potential security incidents immediately.
